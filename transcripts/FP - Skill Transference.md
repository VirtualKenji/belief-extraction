So why is being a generalist better than being a specialist? I've noticed that when all I thought about was writing, my view of the world was very narrow - it was always "what could I write about?" "How do I make this hook better?" "What makes viral content?"

And I did think about this a distribution, but it wasn't that nuanced.

As my knowledge of marketing and strategy and crypto strategy has improved, so has my way of seeing the world.

I actually noticed something interesting: as I age, I'm learning faster and faster, not slower and slower.

But I think the reason why that's happening is basically I have more than one domain expertise at this point. I can write, I can do marketing, I know the whole dating thing and relationship thing as well. Those three all intersect.

So Joey is wondering how come he says things around these like the young models and it sounds stupid, and I don't game but I don't say anything it doesn't sound stupid right?

And I think part of the reason is skill transference. So even if I'm not gaming, the skills I use in my relationship carry over to how it is just talking to a beautiful woman in real life. What skills are those? They are:

- Setting boundaries
- Articulating expected responses
- Reward/punishment
- Compliance
- Not overinvesting

There are a lot of things that just carry over. If there are domain expertise vs not, that's a really short thing.

Okay, so how do I apply that to business? It's really similar to what I just said about skill transference being good for business. That's kind of like an obvious thing, right? Like, of course it's good.

This is actually interesting. Something I've read is that everyone that's really good at business (not everyone, but a lot of people) are like all these good founders and stuff. They're really good at a bunch of things - they know how to sell, they know the market, they know the right distribution funnels, they don't do email marketing (they know to read CTAs), they know how to build all them of tech right.

And I actually don't think that's coincidental. Like, I don't know any really good person I look up to, really good founder, where it seems like he just knows how to do architecture. This is actually true. He knows how to sell, he has a pitch, you know? So do a lot of things. He does have soft skills when it's not the family.

So it's something I've noticed when we backward engineer the most successful people and the people look up to. There's never successful because of just one skill. Even mom is like that - she is a client mom who really understands game theory. In a weird way, where I mean I think she's nice but I think the other reason she puts Jana and the kids of the maids of school is like it is a game theory retention mechanism essentially.

Something to consider is that I think skill transference is the way forward. I think being good at one domain caps potential, and everybody that's ever done something really good is amazing at multiple domains - not just okay, but actually good at multiple domains. That's much better.
